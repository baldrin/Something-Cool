# Copper: you change what is official

**Brainstorm only.** Nothing here is decided. See [the brainstorm index](README.md).

A White decides what something *means*. A Copper decides what is *on record*: who exists, who owns what, who has permission and who is under arrest. Society runs on paperwork, and anything that is official gets treated as true by Grays, Blues, computers and minor officials until someone with more authority checks.

**GM question:** What does the record say, and what's missing?

## Out of combat

- **Forms.** File a form to make something official: a transit permit, a death certificate, a prisoner transfer. You fill in a Form card with its type, subject, authority (whose seal is on it) and effective date. A form is only as strong as its seal, so collecting seals becomes its own game. You can borrow a Gold's seal, have a Green forge one, or steal one.
- **Processing time.** You control the queue. Add or remove segments on any clock that depends on bureaucracy: an arrest warrant, a trial date, a fleet's deployment orders. Copper is the Color that manipulates clocks. You speed up your allies' paperwork and bury your enemies' warrants in review.
- **Paper ghosts.** Create an identity that exists only on paper: a ghost citizen, ghost employee or ghost ship that your crew can step into. The longer a ghost exists, the more real it becomes. Or do the reverse: erase a person from the record. Their access codes stop working, their rations stop, and their name drops off the guard rosters.
- **Audit.** Every NPC has irregularities in their records, and auditing one makes the GM reveal one. That gives you leverage. When the GM hands you two records, finding where they disagree is a puzzle with a real reward.
- **Jurisdiction.** Move a problem into a different court. A crime on Luna becomes a matter for a Martian court where your ally presides, or an arrest stops because the case is outside the officer's jurisdiction.
- **Chain of custody.** Prisoners, evidence and cargo move along paper trails. One rerouted form sends the captured Red to the prison where your crew is waiting.

## In combat

- **The orders have changed.** Reroute enemy orders. Reinforcements go to the wrong gate, and artillery holds fire pending authorization. This works well with the Gray's question, "What are their orders?"
- **Requisition.** Open Society's own armory with a valid form, in the middle of a fight.
- **Stand down notice.** "This unit's deployment authorization expired at noon." Disciplined Grays follow procedure. Obsidians and Golds don't care.
- **Lockout.** Revoke an enemy's credentials for doors, shields and ships.

## How a Copper differs from a Green

A Green hacks a system, which is fast but sets off alarms. A Copper changes the permissions legally, so the system obeys without complaint. It is slow, but nothing gets flagged.

## Resource: Trail

Every form you file leaves a trace. When the Trail track fills, an enemy auditor starts investigating your crew.

## Weaknesses

- Slow.
- No physical strength.
- Powerless against anyone who simply tears up the form.

## Example: the arrest

Your Red is arrested.

- You audit the arresting officer and learn he has been skimming rations.
- You file a transfer to the miners' guild court. It is slow, three segments.
- Meanwhile, a prisoner transfer with a borrowed Gold seal moves the Red tonight, on a transport assigned to a ghost ship your crew is flying.

## Combos with other Colors

- **White:** a precedent a Copper files into the official code becomes binding law in that jurisdiction, even with no witnesses present. See [White](white.md).
- **Silver:** a contract you file can be enforced by the courts. See [Silver](silver.md).
- **Green:** a Green forges the seal, and you file the form that uses it.
