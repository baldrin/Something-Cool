# Brown and Pink

**Brainstorm only.** Nothing here is decided. See [the brainstorm index](README.md).

## Brown: you are already in the room

Everyone else has to get in. A Brown is already inside, because a servant is in every important building. A Brown's power is presence and access.

**GM question:** What do the servants know?

### Out of combat

- **Already there.** Place a servant token on the map anywhere servants would plausibly be, such as a kitchen, a corridor or a private suite. Then you're there. A flashback moves you through time; this moves you through space. You get a set number of uses per operation.
- **Cover task.** Highborns simply don't register you while you're performing service. Each turn, describe what you're doing: carrying a tray, polishing silver, turning down a bed. If the task is believable, you stay Unseen. Break the role (speak out of turn, stand somewhere servants never go, get seen in the same place twice) and Attention rises.
- **Double duty.** Every act of service has a hidden second purpose. You pour the wine, and you also see who has had too much. You take a coat and lift its keycard. You clean the study and copy the documents. Each service lets you take one hidden action, and the GM only checks whether anyone noticed if you fail.
- **The network.** Browns in different households know each other. Each household you serve in becomes a node on a network map. You can send messages through the network slowly and invisibly, and warnings come back to you: "The cook says the guard rotation changed."
- **Routines.** Ask the GM about a household's routines and get a true answer: when the master sleeps, which door sticks, which guard drinks.
- **The kitchen.** In downtime, prepare a feast that sedates or loosens tongues, or poison a single dish.
- **Scapegoat.** Take the blame for a crewmate's mistake. People believe it, because Browns always get blamed. It is dark, but true to the setting, and it costs you.

### In combat

- **In position.** You were already in the room, so you act before the fight starts and usually begin behind enemy lines.
- **Carry in.** Golds have to hand over their razors at the door. You smuggled one in under a serving cloth, and you hand it back at exactly the right moment.
- **Clear the way.** Change the battlefield: lock or unlock doors with service keys, put out the lights, spill oil, move the furniture.
- **Shelter.** Hide the wounded in servants' passages. Nobody thinks to search there.

### Resource: Attention

When Attention fills, you're seen, and Browns who get caught are punished harshly.

### Weaknesses

- Low Station means nobody listens to you in public.
- No physical strength.
- You can't act openly as an authority.

### A seventh layer: Access

Brown might deserve a layer of its own in [the layers of the game](all-colors.md#layers-of-the-game): **Access**, meaning where you are and what you can touch.

## Pink: you change what people feel and what they let slip

A Pink is trained to be close to the powerful, to read them and to earn their trust. That makes them the best spies in Society. These notes keep the focus on trust, reading people and dignity, and keep the rest offscreen.

**GM question:** What are they hiding?

### Out of combat

- **Tells.** Read an NPC to learn their tell, such as "he touches his ring when he lies." Write it on a Tell card. From then on, the GM has to perform the tell whenever that NPC lies, so the whole table can catch it as it happens. Everyone gets to play detective.
- **Trust.** Each important NPC has a Trust track: Stranger, Familiar, Confidant, Devoted. A Confidant tells you secrets without being asked. Someone Devoted will act for you, even against their own interests. Nobody else at the table can move these tracks as fast as you.
- **Mirror.** Become what a target wants. You hold Persona cards, such as "To Lord Arcos, I am a shy poet." A persona gives you an edge with its target, but personas can't overlap. If two targets who know you as different people end up in the same room, you have to keep up both personas at once.
- **Whisper.** Plant an idea in an NPC's mind that comes back later as their own. Write it on a card and give it to the GM. When the moment comes, and if the NPC trusts you enough, the GM has to have them say it. The table watches for it the way it waits for a trap to spring.
- **Redirect.** Shift how an NPC feels about a third person: make them jealous, suspicious or charmed. In a social front, you move the room without ever speaking to the target.
- **Private hours.** Get access to where the powerful relax and speak freely: late at night, in private rooms, alone. You don't sneak in. You're invited.

### In combat

- **Inside the guard.** Nobody guards against you. One strike from intimate range, such as an embrace or a hand on the arm, gets past armor.
- **All eyes on me.** Draw every eye in the room for a moment with a scream, a faint or a scene. Every crewmate gets a hidden action that round.
- **Plea.** Make one attacker hesitate and stop a single strike, if they trust you or are susceptible.
- **Who will switch?** Read which enemy is about to betray their side. This is perfect bait for a Silver's offer.

### Resource: Self

Holding personas wears down who you really are. You recover by spending downtime with your real bonds, the crew. It is a quiet, thematic cost.

### Weaknesses

- You're treated as property.
- The attention you get is dangerous.
- Your access lasts only as long as the powerful want you near.

### Example: the chief of staff

- You learn the rival's chief of staff touches his ring when he lies, so the table catches every lie he tells.
- You whisper "your lord doubts you" into his mind. Two sessions later, he voices that doubt at the worst possible moment for the rival.
- During the escape, All eyes on me gives your Brown and Green their hidden moves.

## Brown and Pink together

The Pink gets invited into the private room, and the Brown is already there, dusting. The Pink draws every eye while the Brown hands the Gold a razor.
