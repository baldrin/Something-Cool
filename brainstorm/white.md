# White: you change the rules the world agrees to follow

**Brainstorm only.** Nothing here is decided. See [the brainstorm index](README.md).

A Gold changes what happens. A White decides what it *means*: whether it is lawful, honorable, sacred or valid. Every White power works only on people who believe in Society or fear it, and only when someone is there to witness it. A White's strength depends on the audience and the target's beliefs, not on the White's own stats.

**GM question:** What binds them?

## Out of combat

- **Pronouncement.** Once per scene, state a law for the scene: "No blade will be drawn in this hall." "This duel ends at first blood." Anyone can still break it, but they pay in Glory or broken Duty, and their own people hesitate. The law binds your crew too, so the skill is writing laws that hurt the enemy more than you.
- **Witness.** Anything said in front of you can become binding. An NPC boasts, "I'd never harm the envoy." You say "Witnessed," and now it is an oath with consequences. Your main tool is listening closely to what NPCs say.
- **The Question.** Ask the GM "What does Society require of this person right now?" and get a true answer: their Duties, oaths and the things they can't be seen doing. If you know a Gold must attend a funeral tomorrow, you can plan an ambush or a pardon.
- **Precedent.** Each ruling you make that holds gets written on a card, like "A lowColor who saves a highColor's life may speak at their trial." Cite it later for a strong effect. Your power grows from the campaign's own history, not from levels. Enemies can cite your precedents against you.
- **Rites.** Slow rituals whose force comes from belief. A funeral rite pauses a blood feud. A marriage rite binds two Houses. An unnaming rite strips a disgraced Gold of their name in public. Each needs preconditions (a body, witnesses, a date), so the crew has to set it up.
- **Confession.** The GM passes you secrets in private notes. Your vow forbids revealing them, but you can act on them. You end up guiding the crew without being able to explain why: "Trust me. We don't board that ship."
- **Oaths.** A teammate swears an oath before you, such as "I will see the Archgovernor fall." While they pursue it, they get an edge. If they break it, the cost is heavy and public.

## In combat

- **Sanctuary.** Declare the ground around you holy. Attacking anyone inside it costs honor, and the zone moves with you. You can't swing a razor, but you can shield the Yellow while she works.
- **Halt.** Call for a parley. Every honorable enemy has to decide whether to keep fighting in front of a White.
- **Name the Crime.** Publicly declare an enemy's act a violation, like striking someone who yielded. Their own Grays and Obsidians hesitate.
- **Proclaim.** When a fight is nearly decided, declare it over. If the situation supports you, the losing side yields with its honor intact. This can save a crewmate who is about to die.

## Resource: Neutrality

Your authority comes from being seen as above the conflict. Each visible act of taking sides spends some of it. With high Neutrality your rulings bind everyone. With low Neutrality only your allies listen. So the way to play is to help your crew without being seen helping. In a rebellion campaign, you are weakest against the very rebels you might want to join.

## Weaknesses

- Useless against people who reject Society, such as rebels, pirates, and Obsidians who follow their own gods.
- Needs witnesses.
- No physical strength.
- Getting caught in a lie ruins you.

## Example: the bridge

A Gray squad holds a bridge. You use the Question and learn the sergeant's brother died on that bridge last week. The crew's Red begins the funeral song for the fallen. You declare a funeral rite and Sanctuary over the mourners. Now the sergeant has to choose between his orders and firing on a rite in front of a White, and the crew walks across.

## Developed further

### Pronouncement

State a law for the scene, phrased as a rule about conduct ("No one will...", "Whoever does X must..."). It can't state facts, so "the door is open" doesn't work.

- **One law at a time.** Stating a new one ends the old one.
- **Force comes from the audience.** The more people present who respect Society, the more it costs to break your law. In an empty room it means almost nothing, so you'll want an audience.
- **Breaking it.** Anyone can break it. They lose Glory or standing, and their own people hesitate.
- **It binds your crew too.** If a crewmate breaks it, your next Pronouncement has no force.
- **Loopholes work both ways.** You say "No blade will be drawn in this hall," and the assassin switches to poison. The White becomes a lawyer playing against the GM's lawyers, and the wording decides who wins.

Some ways to use it:

- "No one leaves this hall until the vote is counted." The rival was planning to slip out.
- "Whoever speaks first after the bell must speak the truth." You already know who is going to speak.
- "Any blade drawn here is drawn against Society." You use this at a feast where you expect an assassination.

### Witness

When someone in your presence makes a promise, denial or claim, you can say "Witnessed." That turns their words into an oath.

- If they later act against it, that is public perjury. They lose Glory, allies distance themselves, and you can name the crime in front of everyone.
- **Limited slots.** You can hold about three witnessed oaths at once, so choose carefully.
- **The choice:** witnessing out loud means they know they're bound. Witnessing in silence means they don't know, but the oath is weaker.
- **Flavor:** NPCs who know how Whites work choose their words carefully around you. Your presence changes how people talk.
- **For allies:** witness a crewmate's testimony to give it legal weight. A Red's word means little in a Gold court until a White stands behind it.

**Combo:** Pronounce "Whoever denies the charge must swear it before me." Witness the denial. When the truth comes out, the denial becomes a Precedent.

### Precedent cards

When you rule on a dispute and your ruling holds by the end of the scene, write it on an index card.

| Card part | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Title | The Right of the Rescuer |
| Ruling | A lowColor who saves a highColor's life may speak at their trial. |
| Case | Brown steward Hollis, Luna, the gala fire |
| Weight | Tally marks, one for each time it has been upheld |

- **Citing.** When a situation matches, cite the card. The GM treats it as the default ruling unless someone challenges it. Each successful citation adds a tally mark, and heavier precedents are harder to overturn.
- **Challenges.** An enemy White or Copper can challenge a card. That becomes a courtroom front in an operation.
- **It is public.** The deck sits in the middle of the table, and enemies can cite your cards against you. Other players can spot matching situations too, so the whole table plays the deck.
- **Your growth is the deck.** Instead of gaining levels, you build a legal code over the campaign. Late in a campaign, a heavy deck can change a city's laws, like "lowColors may own property here." A White can break chains legally, one ruling at a time.

### Confession notes

The GM hands you secrets on folded cards. Your vow means you can't reveal them in words, hints or writing. You can act on them yourself.

- **Counsel tokens.** To keep the secrets from cutting you off from the group, you get a few tokens each session. Give one to a teammate with an instruction: "Don't board that ship." "Trust the Brown." If they follow it without asking why, they get a bonus. If they ask why or refuse, the token comes back to you. Trusting the White becomes part of how the crew plays.
- **Hearing confessions** is a downtime action. The GM gives you one to three notes. Some are trivial and one may change everything.
- **Breaking the vow** is possible, but your Pronouncement and Witness stop working for the rest of the arc. The GM can tempt you with secrets that make breaking it very attractive.
- **Payoff.** At the end of an arc, open the old notes so the group finally learns why you said what you said.

## Combos with other Colors

- **Silver:** a contract blessed by a White can't be outbid by anyone honorable. A Silver finds a witness's price, and the White makes their testimony binding. See [Silver](silver.md).
- **Copper:** a precedent a Copper files into the official code becomes binding law in that jurisdiction, even with no witnesses present. See [Copper](copper.md).
