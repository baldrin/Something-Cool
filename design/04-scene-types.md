# 04 Scene types

This doc lists the kinds of scenes a campaign will have, shows which Colors contribute where, and sets out the main design proposal: **operations**.

## Scene catalog

Each scene type includes a few sample costs to use on a partial success, so the GM does not have to invent every cost during play (lesson 6 in [02 Borrowed mechanics](02-borrowed-mechanics.md)).

| Scene | What happens | Typical fronts | Sample costs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Battle** | A ground fight, from a street skirmish to an assault on a citadel. | The line, the systems, the wounded, behind the lines | Take a wound, lose ground, a weapon breaks, an ally is cut off |
| **Ship battle** | A fight between ships or on a ship being boarded. | Helm, engines, systems, boarding | Hull breach, a system goes dark, the enemy gains position |
| **Duel** | A formal or sudden one on one fight, usually with razors. | The duel, the crowd, the terms, the seconds | A scar, lost composure, the crowd turns, a rule is broken |
| **Infiltration** | Getting into, through or out of a guarded place. | Entry, security, the target, the exit | An alarm clock fills, a disguise slips, a path closes |
| **Gold society** | A gala, feast, hunt or summit where Golds scheme in public. | The floor, the private rooms, below stairs | Offend a host, owe a favor, be seen with the wrong person |
| **Court and ceremony** | A trial, Senate debate, House council or rite. | The chamber, the evidence, the crowd | A ruling goes against you, a witness is lost, a precedent is set |
| **Investigation** | Learning who did what, where something is, or what someone plans. | Records, witnesses, the scene, the network | Tip off the target, find a false lead, spend time you lack |
| **Negotiation** | Trading terms with a rival, ally or patron. | The table, the leverage, the alternatives | Give up more than you meant to, show your hand, make an enemy |
| **Travel and survival** | Crossing space, ice, desert, ruins or the deep mines. | The route, supplies, the environment, pursuers | Lose supplies, take exposure, arrive late, be tracked |
| **Rally** | Moving a crowd, crew or population to act. | The message, the crowd, the opposition | The crowd turns violent, a leader is marked, the message spreads too far |
| **Downtime** | Time between operations: healing, training, building, scheming. | Personal projects | Time passes and rivals act, a resource is used up |

### What the matrix shows

Every Color leads in at least one scene that is not a fight. The gaps are in the scenes where only a few Colors fit:

| Scene | Colors with no natural role | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Battle** | Silver, White, Copper, Violet, Brown, Pink | Combat is the group's favorite part, so six Colors with nothing to do in a fight is the biggest problem on this page. |
| **Duel** | Ten of fourteen | A duel is one on one by nature. Everyone else watches. |
| **Ship battle** | Silver, White, Copper, Violet, Obsidian, Brown, Pink, Red | Ship battles risk becoming the Blue's solo game. |
| **Court and ceremony** | Every lowColor, plus Blue, Green and Violet | lowColors often cannot even speak in a Gold court. |

A rule that asks "who is good at this scene" will always leave someone out. The proposal below asks a different question: **what are all the things happening in this scene at once?**

## Proposal: operations

An **operation** is any high stakes scene run in rounds, like combat, with several fronts happening at the same time. A battle is an operation. So is a gala, a trial or a mine rescue. Every player acts every round, on whichever front their character can affect.

### How it works

1. **Stakes.** Before it starts, the group and the GM say what each side wants and what losing means. Losing rarely means death. It can mean exile, debt, exposure or a lost ally. (From Burning Wheel's Duel of Wits and Dune.)
2. **Fronts.** The GM sets two to four fronts. Each front is a place or an approach, with a **progress clock** for what the crew wants and a **danger clock** for what the opposition is doing. (Clocks from Blades in the Dark.)
3. **Rounds.** Every player acts once per round, the way they do in combat. A round lasts as long as the scene needs: seconds in a fight, an hour at a gala.
4. **Actions.** On their turn, a player can:
   - **Act on a front** to fill its progress clock.
   - **Help** a teammate: set them up, assist their roll, or protect them from a consequence. (From Blades in the Dark.)
   - **Create an advantage** that any teammate can use, even on a different front. (From Fate.)
   - **Flash back** to show how they prepared for this moment, at a cost. (From Blades in the Dark and Leverage.)
5. **Fronts connect.** An advantage made on one front can be spent on another. This is how a Brown in the kitchens helps a Gold in the ballroom.
6. **The opposition moves.** At the end of each round, the GM fills danger clocks based on misses and costs, and may add a new front.
7. **Ending.** The operation ends when the main progress clock fills, when a danger clock fills and forces a retreat or a new problem, or when one side yields.

### Guardrails from the research

- **Small moments are one roll.** Operations are only for high stakes scenes. Using the full structure for minor moments is slow (lesson 2 in [02 Borrowed mechanics](02-borrowed-mechanics.md)).
- **No solo subsystems.** A Green's hacking is a front in the same round, not a separate game (lesson 1).
- **Every front has more than one way in.** Help is never worse than doing nothing (lesson 3).
- **Same roll everywhere.** Combat and non-combat scenes use the same core roll and round structure, so switching between them does not feel like switching games (Lancer's lesson).

### Closing the gaps

Here is how operations give each gap Color a job.

**Battle.** The fight is one front among several.
- *The line:* Gold, Gray, Obsidian.
- *The systems* (shields, turrets, doors): Green, Orange.
- *The wounded:* Yellow.
- *The orders:* Copper forges new orders, Silver buys off the mercenaries, White calls for a parley.
- *Behind the lines:* Brown and Pink are already inside and can open the gate or reach the commander.
- *The crowd:* Violet broadcasts, Red rallies the workers.

**Duel.** The duel itself is short. Around it:
- *The terms:* White sets them, and someone may try to bend them.
- *The crowd and the bets:* Silver and Violet.
- *The seconds and the sabotage:* Brown, Pink and Green.
- *The healer:* Yellow.
- *The way out if it goes wrong:* Blue and Orange.

**Ship battle.** Every station on the ship is a front:
- *Helm:* Blue.
- *Engines and damage control:* Orange.
- *Systems:* Green.
- *Medbay:* Yellow.
- *Boarding:* Gold, Gray, Obsidian.
- *Signals:* Copper and Violet fake a ship's identity.
- *The lowColor crew below decks:* Red and Brown keep them working, or turn them.

**Court and ceremony.** The chamber is one front. The others are outside it:
- *Evidence:* Green, Yellow, Brown, Pink.
- *The crowd at the doors:* Violet and Red.
- *The threat of violence:* Gray and Obsidian guard against, or provide, a sudden end to the proceedings.

### Example: the gala

**Setup.** A Gold House is hosting a gala at its summer palace on Luna. The crew needs a vote from the House elders, and proof of a rival's crimes from the House archive.

**The crew.** A Gold heir, a Pink, a Green, a Brown and an Obsidian bodyguard.

**Fronts.**

| Front | Progress clock | Danger clock | Who works it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The ballroom | Win the elders' vote (8) | The rival sways the elders (6) | Gold, Pink |
| Below stairs | Reach the archive (6) | The steward grows suspicious (4) | Brown |
| Security hub | Loop the cameras (4) | A Green on the host's staff notices (4) | Green |
| The garden gate | Keep the exit clear (4) | Patrols close the gate (6) | Obsidian |

**Round one.**
- The Green loops one camera and creates an advantage: *blind corridor*.
- The Brown spends *blind corridor* on the below stairs front and moves two segments closer to the archive.
- The Pink reads an elder and creates an advantage: *the elder fears the rival's debts*.
- The Gold spends that advantage on the ballroom front for a strong pitch.
- The Obsidian, who has no part in the talking, keeps the patrols away from the garden gate, so the escape route stays open.

Nobody waited. Every player did something that mattered to the outcome, and three of the five helped someone else.
