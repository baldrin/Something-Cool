# 01 Goals

These goals come from the group's answers. Every rule we write should serve at least one of them. When two goals conflict, the one higher on the list wins.

## G1. Everyone contributes in every scene

This is the top goal.

The group loves combat because everyone gets a turn and a job. Outside combat, some players have nothing to do. The game should fix that by giving every Color a real way to contribute in every kind of high stakes scene, and by making sure no single player's task leaves the rest of the table waiting.

**Test:** in any high stakes scene, every player can name one useful thing their character could do this round.

## G2. Combat stays exciting and tactical

Combat is still the group's favorite part. Fights should feel fast and dangerous, positioning should matter, and teamwork should pay off. Non-fighters should have useful jobs inside a fight too: hacking shields, keeping people alive, flying the ship, opening the exit.

**Test:** a fight where only the Gold and the Obsidian matter is a broken fight.

## G3. Character building is deep

The group loves making characters. Creation and growth should involve real choices with tradeoffs: Color, upbringing, training, gear, bonds, and the drives that push a character forward.

**Test:** two characters of the same Color can feel very different.

## G4. The Color hierarchy feels real

Society treats you according to your Color, and the rules reflect that. Golds are stronger, and other Colors are overlooked, underestimated and trusted with things Golds never see. Every Color matters, but not in the same way.

**Test:** where your character can go and who will listen to them depends on their Color.

## G5. The books' themes come through in play

The rules should push play toward the things that make Red Rising feel like Red Rising:

- loyalty and betrayal
- ambition and the cost of power
- breaking chains, both literal and social
- plans revealed only when they pay off
- pride, honor and the duels that come from them

**Test:** after a session, the group's story could sit next to the books in tone.

## G6. Easy for 5e players to learn

The group already knows 5e. Where a 5e habit works, keep it. Where we change something, there should be a clear reason tied to one of the goals above.

**Test:** a player can make a character and play a first scene after a short explanation.

## What to keep from 5e

- **Character building** with meaningful choices at creation and as characters grow.
- **Turn based tactical combat** where everyone acts and positioning matters.
- **Advantage and disadvantage,** a simple and popular way to apply circumstances to a roll.
- **Backgrounds** that give a character history and skills beyond their combat role.

## What to fix from 5e

From the group:

- **Players with nothing to do.** Combat gives everyone a turn. Most other scenes do not.

Suggested, not yet confirmed by the group:

- **One roll and done.** Outside combat, an important moment often comes down to a single check. High stakes scenes need more structure than that, and small moments need less.
- **Thin support for intrigue.** Red Rising is full of politics, deals, spying and betrayal. Those need real rules.
- **Late game power creep.** Characters should keep growing without making challenges meaningless.

## Non-goals

- Simulating every detail of the setting's technology or economy.
- Retelling the books' plot. Our story takes place in its own era.
