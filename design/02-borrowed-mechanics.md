# 02 Borrowed mechanics

A survey of mechanics from other RPGs that players like, grouped by the goal from [01 Goals](01-goals.md) they would serve. Each entry covers what the mechanic is, why players like it, how it could fit Red Rising, and its risks.

Nothing here is adopted yet. Every entry is a **candidate**.

**About the sources.** Most of what players say about these games lives in forum threads, blog posts and reviews, not formal surveys. Treat the "why players like it" notes as patterns from those discussions. The source links came from a web search, and not every page could be opened to check it, so read them before relying on a specific claim. Entries marked *not researched this round* are described from the games' rules, without a source check on player opinion. All descriptions are in our own words.

## Lessons from the research

These six lessons shaped the proposals in [04 Scene types](04-scene-types.md).

1. **A separate subsystem for one player leaves the rest of the table waiting.** This is the most common complaint about Shadowrun's deckers. Anything one Color does alone, such as a Green's hacking, should happen in the same rounds as everyone else's actions.
2. **One framework for every conflict works, but it is slow for small things.** Dune and Mouse Guard use the same structure for fights, arguments and intrigue. Players like that, and reviews also say it drags when used for minor moments. Keep the full structure for high stakes scenes.
3. **Group challenges fail when only the right specialists can help.** The main complaint about 4e skill challenges is that one or two characters do the real work, and others feel their help is more likely to hurt. Every front of a scene needs several ways in, and helping should never be worse than doing nothing.
4. **Clocks and flashbacks are the mechanics players most often want in other games.** Both are strong fits for Red Rising's long plans and late reveals.
5. **A shared resource is good, but keep it to one currency.** Dune reviewers found its currency rules overcomplicated.
6. **Partial success needs guidance.** "Success with a cost" keeps play moving, but GMs report it is hard to invent good costs in the middle of play. Each scene type should come with a short list of costs.

## G1. Everyone contributes

### Teamwork actions (Blades in the Dark)
*Not researched this round.*
- **What it is.** Named ways to help: assist a teammate's roll for a personal cost, set up a teammate so their next action is safer or stronger, protect a teammate by taking the consequence for them, or lead a group action.
- **Fit for Red Rising.** Very strong. A Brown who unlocks a door sets up the Gray who goes through it. A Yellow who keeps a wounded Obsidian on their feet assists. Teamwork matches the Howlers and the Sons of Ares.
- **Risk.** Players may help instead of acting, which slows rounds. Each type of help needs a cost.

### Create an advantage (Fate)
*Not researched this round.*
- **What it is.** Any character can spend an action to add a new fact to the scene (such as "the guards are drunk" or "the shield generator is flickering") that a teammate can then use for a bonus.
- **Fit for Red Rising.** This gives a non-fighter a real action in a fight or a duel: a Green flickers the shields and a Pink distracts the target, then the Gold strikes.
- **Risk.** Without limits, the table spends rounds building advantages instead of acting on them.

### Flashbacks (Blades in the Dark, Leverage)
- **What it is.** In the middle of a scene, a player says "I prepared for this," and the group plays out a short scene from earlier to show how. A roll or a cost decides whether the preparation worked.
- **Why players like it.** It ends long planning sessions and makes characters look competent. Some players argue it works in any genre where the characters should look capable, not only in heists.
- **Fit for Red Rising.** Excellent. The books are full of plans revealed only at the moment they pay off. Flashbacks let any player, of any Color, contribute a twist.
- **Risk.** Needs a cost so players cannot prepare for everything.
- **Sources.** [Gnome Stew](https://gnomestew.com/three-mechanics-from-blades-in-the-dark-i-want-in-all-my-games/), [Critical Hits review of Leverage](https://critical-hits.com/2010/11/26/review-leverage-rpg)

### Progress clocks (Blades in the Dark)
- **What it is.** A circle cut into four, six or eight segments. Fill segments as progress is made or danger grows. When the clock fills, the thing happens.
- **Why players like it.** It is the mechanic people most often say they now use in every game. It makes progress and danger visible to everyone at once.
- **Fit for Red Rising.** Excellent. Uses include a carved character's exposure, a Proctor's attention, a fleet's arrival, a Senate vote, and a mine's air supply. Clocks are also the natural way to track each front in an operation.
- **Risk.** Too many clocks at once are hard to follow.
- **Sources.** [Gnome Stew](https://gnomestew.com/three-mechanics-from-blades-in-the-dark-i-want-in-all-my-games/), [Mechanics to Steal: Clocks](https://sidrdesai.substack.com/p/mechanics-to-steal-clocks)

### Crew roles (Leverage)
- **What it is.** Each player takes a role, such as the fighter, the hacker, the con artist, the thief or the planner. Each role has a clear job in every job the crew takes.
- **Why players like it.** Reviewers call the roles the core of the design because they decide what each player does in a scene.
- **Fit for Red Rising.** Good, with a twist. Colors already suggest roles, but a role should not be locked to a Color. A Red can be the thief and a Pink can be the planner.
- **Risk.** Leverage works best with exactly five players, one per role. Our structure must work with any group size.
- **Sources.** [GMS Magazine review](https://www.gmsmagazine.com/leverage-the-rpg-review/), [Critical Hits review](https://critical-hits.com/2010/11/26/review-leverage-rpg)

### One framework for every conflict (Dune: Adventures in the Imperium, Mouse Guard)
- **What it is.** Dune runs duels, skirmishes, warfare, espionage and intrigue with the same core rules. Mouse Guard does the same for fights, arguments, chases and journeys.
- **Why players like it.** Social and mental contests get the same weight as fights, and defeat does not have to mean death: rivals can yield, be exiled or lose their resources.
- **Fit for Red Rising.** Very strong. Dune is the closest setting in tone: noble houses, duels, spies and politics. This is the base of the **operation** proposal in [04 Scene types](04-scene-types.md).
- **Risk.** Reviews of Mouse Guard note that using the full structure for every argument is slow. Keep it for high stakes scenes.
- **Sources.** [Modiphius design blog](https://modiphius.net/blogs/news/dune-2-gaming-in-the-imperium), [Roll20 Dune compendium: The Nature of Conflict](https://roll20.net/compendium/DUNE/The%20Nature%20of%20Conflict), [RPG.net review of Mouse Guard](https://dev.rpg.net/reviews/archive/14/14195.phtml)

### Shared team pool (Dune Momentum, Masks Team)
- **What it is.** Good rolls add points to a pool the whole group shares. Anyone can spend from it to boost a roll. In Masks, spending team points on yourself means ignoring your teammates.
- **Why players like it.** One player's good roll helps everyone, so players care about each other's turns.
- **Fit for Red Rising.** Good. It suits a crew that wins or dies together. Masks' version, where taking for yourself has a social cost, fits the books' ambitious characters.
- **Risk.** Dune reviewers found its currency rules overcomplicated and the dice bookkeeping distracting. Keep one simple pool.
- **Sources.** [Black Campbell review](https://blackcampbell.com/2024/05/11/review-dune-adventures-in-the-imperium-rpg), [Cannibal Halfling Gaming review](https://cannibalhalflinggaming.com/?p=6656), [Masks discussion on Paizo forums](https://paizo.com/campaigns/MasksANewGenerationOfHeroes)

### Group challenges (D&D 4e skill challenges)
- **What it is.** The party works toward a number of successes before a number of failures, with each player using skills.
- **Why players like it.** When it works, it pulls the whole party into non-combat scenes. Some GMs say these were among the best sessions they ran.
- **Why it often fails.** Only the one or two characters with the right skills do the real work, and others feel their rolls are more likely to hurt the group than help it.
- **Fit for Red Rising.** Use it as a warning more than a model. Its lesson is part of the operation proposal: several fronts, several ways in, and help that never makes things worse.
- **Sources.** [EN World: skill challenges as a core subsystem](https://www.enworld.org/goto/post?id=5275725), [EN World: an exercise in dice rolling?](https://www.enworld.org/threads/4e-skill-challenges-an-exercise-in-dice-rolling.246093/)

## G2. Tactical combat

### Advantage and disadvantage (D&D 5e)
*Not researched this round.*
- **What it is.** Roll two dice and keep the better or worse result, instead of tracking many small bonuses.
- **Fit for Red Rising.** The group already knows it, so it is worth keeping if the dice core allows it. With a d20 core it carries over directly.

### Narrative mode and tactical mode (Lancer)
- **What it is.** Light rules for scenes outside a fight, and a detailed tactical mode for combat.
- **Why players like it.** Reviewers praise the combat as tactical and brutal with little waiting between turns.
- **Why some tables dislike it.** The switch between modes creates friction, and the narrative side feels thin compared with combat.
- **Fit for Red Rising.** Take the tactical depth, but avoid the split. Use the same core roll and round structure in and out of combat so nothing feels like a different game.
- **Sources.** [TTRPG Factory review](https://ttrpgfactory.com/2023/03/01/lancer-rpg-review/), [Shacknews review](https://shacknews.com/cortex/article/138/mud-lasers-and-giant-robots-a-review-of-lancer-the-mech-rpg), [EN World: is Lancer any good?](https://www.enworld.org/threads/is-lancer-any-good.701686/latest)

### Strife and duels (Legend of the Five Rings, 5th edition)
*Not researched this round.*
- **What it is.** Characters build up strife from insults, fear and pressure. Past their limit, they lose composure in public, which costs them socially. The game also has dedicated rules for formal duels.
- **Fit for Red Rising.** Strong for Golds, whose honor culture makes composure and insults matter. A strife track also gives a duel stakes beyond hit points: you can lose by losing your temper.

## G3. Character building

### Lifepath creation (Traveller)
- **What it is.** You build a character by playing through their past: upbringing, career terms, events, and what they gained or lost along the way.
- **Why players like it.** Players praise it for producing characters with history built in.
- **Fit for Red Rising.** Very strong. Red Rising lives are shaped by where you were born, how you were schooled (the Institute, the Academy, the mines) and where you were first posted. A lifepath also handles carving well: one step of your past can be the carving.
- **Risk.** Random lifepaths can produce characters players don't want. Offer a choose-your-path version.
- **Sources.** [EN World: tell me about your favorite mechanics](https://www.enworld.org/goto/post?id=8920435)

### Drives that pay out (Burning Wheel Beliefs)
*Not researched this round.*
- **What it is.** Each player writes a few short statements about what their character believes and intends to do. Acting on them, or struggling with them, earns rewards used for advancement.
- **Fit for Red Rising.** Strong. Characters in the books are driven by single burning goals. Growth tied to drives also supports a hybrid advancement system (see O2 in [00 Decisions](00-decisions.md)).

## G4. The hierarchy feels real

### Honor, glory and status (Legend of the Five Rings)
- **What it is.** Separate scores for personal integrity, public reputation and social rank. Failing in public can cost all three.
- **Fit for Red Rising.** Very strong, with a twist. **Status** is mostly fixed by Color, so a Red stays low no matter what. **Glory** (renown) can grow for anyone, which is how a Red becomes a legend. **Honor** matters most to Golds and Obsidians. The gap between status and glory is itself a story.
- **Sources.** [Gaming Trend review of the core rulebook](https://gamingtrend.com/reviews/honor-and-steel-legend-of-the-five-rings-core-rulebook-and-game-masters-kit-review)

### Passions and noble houses (Pendragon)
*Not researched this round.*
- **What it is.** Characters have strong passions, such as loyalty to a lord or hatred of a rival, that can push them to great deeds at a risk. Play can span generations of a noble family.
- **Fit for Red Rising.** Strong for Gold Houses and their households. Passions also fit lowColors: love of family, hatred of Gold. Generational play fits a long campaign in our own era.

## G5. The books' themes

### Social combat (Burning Wheel Duel of Wits)
- **What it is.** A structured debate where both sides state what they want before it begins and must accept the result.
- **Why players like it.** Players call it as engaging and strategic as combat, but with words. Both sides agree to the stakes, and the loser can still get a compromise.
- **Risk.** Players say it is tedious for minor disputes. It is best saved for big moments.
- **Fit for Red Rising.** Strong for Senate debates, trials and Gold negotiations. It can be one front of an operation.
- **Sources.** [Burning Wheel forums: why have social combat?](https://forums.burningwheel.com/t/why-is-having-a-social-combat-system-a-good-thing/11596)

### Bonds that wear down (Delta Green)
- **What it is.** Characters have relationships with scores. Under pressure, a character can protect themselves by letting a relationship take the damage.
- **Why players like it.** Reviewers call it elegant and tragic because it forces a choice between your own stability and the people you love.
- **Fit for Red Rising.** Very strong. It fits loyalty and betrayal: a character can survive by sacrificing a friendship, and that cost lasts.
- **Sources.** [Unwinnable on Delta Green](https://unwinnable.com/2021/07/19/delta-green-2016/), [RPG.net review](https://dev.rpg.net/reviews/archive/16/16924.phtml)

### Influence (Masks: A New Generation)
- **What it is.** Characters can hold influence over each other. Someone with influence can tell you who you are, and that shifts your stats unless you reject it.
- **Why players like it.** Reviewers praise how it makes identity move during play and pushes the story forward.
- **Fit for Red Rising.** Good for mentors, rivals and patrons, such as a Gold sponsor who shapes a lowColor protégé, or a rival whose insults shake a Gold's confidence.
- **Sources.** [Soapbox review](https://soapbox.manywords.press/?p=3951)

### Partial success (Powered by the Apocalypse)
- **What it is.** Rolls have three results: clean success, success with a cost or hard choice, and a miss where the GM makes a move.
- **Why players like it.** Most rolls give players something, so scenes keep moving instead of stopping at a failure.
- **Risk.** GMs report that inventing a good cost every time is hard work.
- **Fit for Red Rising.** Strong. The books rarely let anyone win cleanly. Each scene type should include a short list of costs to choose from.
- **Sources.** [Wikipedia: Powered by the Apocalypse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powered_by_the_Apocalypse), [The GM burden of adjudicating partial successes](https://drolleries.substack.com/p/the-gm-burden-of-adjudicating-partial), [Daily Apocalypse on failing forward](https://daily-apocalypse.com/daily-apocalypse/143-beyond-success-failure-failing-forward-and-moves-in-apocalypse-world)

### Stress and trauma (Blades in the Dark)
*Not researched this round.*
- **What it is.** Characters spend stress to push themselves or avoid harm. Too much stress causes lasting trauma.
- **Fit for Red Rising.** Good. Every character in the books is pushed past their limits and carries scars. It gives lowColors who cannot match Golds physically a way to push beyond their limits, at a price.

### Aspects (Fate)
*Not researched this round.*
- **What it is.** Short phrases that are true about a character or scene, such as "Sworn to House Augustus." You can invoke one for a bonus, or have it used against you in exchange for a reward.
- **Fit for Red Rising.** Good for Color expectations and House loyalties, which help and hurt in equal measure.

## Things to avoid

### The decker problem (Shadowrun)
Players describe Shadowrun's hacking rules as a separate subsystem that pulls play away from the rest of the table while one player works alone. Some advise making hackers non-player characters for that reason.

**Lesson:** a Green hacking a security system acts in the same rounds as everyone else, rolling against a clock like any other front.

**Sources.** [EN World](https://www.enworld.org/goto/post?id=6278618), [Shadowrun Tabletop forums](https://forums.shadowruntabletop.com/index.php/topic,34641.0/wap2.html)

### Two currencies for one idea (Dune)
**Lesson:** one shared pool, named once, with a short list of uses.

### Partial success without a menu of costs (Powered by the Apocalypse)
**Lesson:** give the GM a list of costs for every scene type.
