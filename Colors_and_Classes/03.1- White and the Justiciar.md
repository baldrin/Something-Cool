# White and the Justiciar

*Golds kill. Whites determine if it was was merit or murder.*

## The Color: White

Whites are the priests and judges of the Society. They keep its rites, preside over its rituals, they sanction its duels, and remember its laws-- even when others find them inconvenient. They are physically unimposing and make for terrible liars. 

While Gold Society has, by social contract codified in the Compact, delegated many tasks and responsibilities to other Colors, they have relinquished very little actual *control* to any other Color. Whites are perhaps the only exception to this. Their adjudications and sanctions are treated with near religious reverence by Golds and other honor-bound members of society. 

In Pre-Fall Society and in the Societal Remnant (c. PCE 744), Whites live under the *Pax Alba* (The White Peace) offering Whites a blanket protection from harm enforced by the Compact. Outside of open hostilities, anyone, including a Peerless, who kills a White functioning within their legal position would be in violation of the Compact and be *ipso facto* declared *hostis publicus* (enemy of the state). In time, the *Pax Alba* extended its definition to include any transgression against an official White ruling. This gives Whites extraordinary power-- their sanction, or lack thereof, marks the difference between one Gold cutting down another being a duel or murder.

After the Fall, the Solar Republic's New Compact offers much of the same general emphasis on White rulings, but without the religious fervor and unquestioning obedience seen in the Societal Remnant. Where in the Society, a White's ruling was tantamount to law, in the Republic, those same rulings are *interpretations* of the law, and therefore more open to debate.

## White Progression

Whites progress through two tiers as they advance in hierarchy.

In Tier 1, they choose background cloister. Each selection provides a specialization that increases the character's ability to impact rolls.

In Tier 2, they take on significantly more influence, but, like balancing the scales of justice, this comes with a significant downside.

## The Signature Class: Justiciar

**Role:** Support and control
**Focus:** WIS, CAS
**Colors:** White only

The Justiciar's weapon is the honor of other people. 

## Class Resources: Neutrality and Authority

The Justiciar must balance two resources. Neutrality, which measures how unbiased you appear and dictates who is controlled by your actions, and Authority, which lends power to your skills. 

### Neutrality

A Justiciar's influence lessens with individuals who don't trust that they're being treated fairly. While from some positions people may accept bias as long as its in their favor, for someone as influential as a Justiciar *any* perceived bias in *any* direction weakens their influence over everyone. High neutrality binds everyone, low neutrality binds only those bound by honor, and eventually binds no one.

- **Meter**: Starts a 0. Gaining neutrality moves the number in a positive direction, leading to your skills affecting more people. Losing neutrality moves the number in a negative direction, progressively limiting who you have influence over.
- **Cap**: 3 x (class tier + 1)
- **Gain Neutrality**: When you publicly make a neutral judgment, accept a surrender, protect a prisoner, or take other actions deemed to demonstrate neutrality at the GM's discretion. Use of the Due Process skill can also increase Neutrality.
- **Lose Neutrality**: When you publicly influence matters for the sake of your party or perform rites, rituals, or judgments that show undue preference for a side.

Neutrality Meter

	<-5:      Skills regarding laws or verdicts no longer affect anyone
	-5 to -1: Affects only honor-bound Colors
	0 to 5:   Affects all law-abiding citizens
	>5:       Affects anyone living within the Society or Republic

### Authority

Where Neutrality defines the reach of a skill, Authority is the currency used to execute on that reach. Authority is staked when using skills. Authority is consumed when an opponent succeeds on a roll against that skill. When there is no Authority left on a skill, the effect ends.

- **Cap.** Set by your class tier.
- **Opening.** You begin each scene with Authority equal to your Color Rank.
- Your allocation is completely filled during Downtime.
- **Gain 1** when your Piercing Deception catches a lie, when you witness an Oath (once per character per scene for Oaths given voluntarily), when you succeed on a WIS, INT, or CAS check, or when any character fails a WIS, INT, or CAS roll against you.
- **Stake.** You Stake Authority on skills. Some skills have set amounts of Authority required to start the skill, others allow a variable amount.
- **Defiance**. Each time a target under effect of a skill defies it by beating the DC, 1 Authority is lost from the stake. The skill ends when there is no Authority staked on it. If Authority is lost due to Defiance, that Authority does not return to your pool. Any Authority staked on a skill that is voluntarily ended returns to your pool.
- **Spend.** Some skills require Authority to be spent directly. A Justiciar may also spend 1 Authority for +2 on a Social check against an honor-bound character.

### Tier 1: Witness

- [[Piercing Deception]] *(Mental action).* Roll d20 + WIS against the target's d20 + GUI. On a success you detect if the target is lying, and the target has disadvantage on GUI checks against the party for the rest of the scene.
- **Sworn In** *(Utility).* Your testimony is accepted as legal fact in any court of the Compact. You may witness an oath or contract. A party who later breaks it becomes Disgraced.
- **Pronouncement** (*Social action*, *stake X*). You can set one new law during a Scene or Encounter. You can only have one law active at a time. The law is implemented as worded and interpreted by the GM and must meet the following requirements:
	  - The Pronouncement must be a one sentence statement.
	  - It must not clearly violate the Compact, physics, or be impossible to follow.
	  - It is only binding to those present who you have the Neutrality score to affect. It is binding to *all* such present, including the party.
	  - If the Pronouncement clearly excludes or targets one group, you lose 10 Neutrality.
	  - The Sentence is the punishment for breaking the Pronouncement and is determined by the *stake*.
		  - *Staked* *1*: *Delictum*: no punishment, only forces the challenge roll
		  - *Staked 2*: *Infamia*: Disgraced condition.
		  - *Staked 5*: *Exilium*: Exiled condition. 
		  - *Staked 8*: *Morte Damnare*: Death Sentence condition.
		  - *Staked 10*: *Ad Necem Petitus*: Enemy of the State condition.
	  - Loopholes are allowed as long as they fit within the exact wording of the Pronouncement
	  - Example: "Any blade drawn here is in violation of the Compact". This binds the party and any opponents from using a knife, sword, or razor. But anyone may pull a scorcher or hammer.
	  - A character attempting to violate a Pronouncement rolls a d20 + COU against the Justiciars d20 + WIS. On a critical success, their attempt succeeds and does not incur the Sentence. On a success, the attempt succeeds but incurs the Sentence. On a failure, their action fails, but does not incur the Sentence. On a critical failure, their action fails and they incur the Sentence. When a character succeeds against the check, 1 stake is removed but the punishment does not change; when they fail, you gain 1 Authority. 
	  - The Pronouncement ends when the stake is exhausted or when the Scene or Encounter ends
  - **Due Process** (Social action). You can name two parties in a dispute; each party may speak or act once without interference. You then give you opinion on who is right, granting advantage on the next roll regarding the outcome of the dispute to the party you agreed with. If you give an opinion that doesn't side with either party, gain 1 Neutrality. If you side against an ally, gain 2 Neutrality.


### Tier 2: Lector

- [[Verdict]] *(Social action, spend 1).* Roll a d20 + CAS (DC 15) to issue a ruling: start a formal duel and assign a champion, call the end to a duel that is already ongoing, accept an honorable surrender, or place a prisoner under protection. A character under your influence who attempts to defy it rolls a d20 + COU against the players CAS roll. On a success, they can defy the Verdict but suffers a permanent -2 loss of LOY and is actively impeded by other characters under your influence who heard the verdict as well and did not succeed on the COU roll.
- **Cross-Examine** *(Mental action, stake 1).* You ask a character a question and roll a d20 + WIS against their d20 + GUI. If you succeed , they must answer truthfully or say nothing at all. On a failure, you spend the staked Authority.
- **Writ** *(Utility, spend 1 Authority, lose 1 Neutrality).* You issue a lawful warrant or writ that legally justifies a suspicious action. Against any entity functioning within the Compact, this lowers the party's Scrutiny by 1. This can also be used to compel a character under your influence to follow your instruction which would otherwise draw suspicion.
- **Confession** (*Utility*, *GM activated*). The GM gives you a secret confession (or multiple confessions) from an NPC. This secret is under a sacramental seal for a set duration tied to an existing Clock. You are allowed to act on this information your own and with your own actions, but you are not allowed to divulge, through words or action, what that secret is. Violation of this Seal reduces the Justiciar's Neutrality to -10 and permanently removes the Confession utility. When asked for an explanation of a Justiciar's action, the Justiciar must reply "I am under Seal" if it could possibly be related to the Confession. It is the GM's sole discretion if the seal has been violated. The GM will indicate when the duration of the seal is over at which point the Justiciar can reveal the contents of the secret to the group.
	- Example: A secret might be given to you that states "An assassin is onboard the *Valorum* transport ship, and they will try to kill Senator Cordus just before his arrival to the Citadel". The Justiciar is not allowed to reveal the plot to the party. The Justiciar can search for the assassin or recommend finding the Senator, but cannot indicate to the party that they need to protect the Senator (directly revealing the presence of a threat) or a that there is an assassin. The Justiciar however might use their Pronouncement to prevent anyone from moving between compartments or use their Writ to force the Senator to enter the cockpit of the ship.
	- Confessions invoke the "I am under seal" response to questions from the group regardless of the triviality of the Confession. For instance, if given the secret "The soup at the gala has a toenail in it," the Justiciar would still be required to say "I am under seal" when your character is asked why they won't eat it.

### Tier 3: Magister

- [[Sanctuary]] *(Mental action, stake 2).* Roll d20 + WIS to consecrate a 20 ft radius space centered on the Justiciar. This is a fixed area that does not follow the Justiciar. Opponents under your influence must beat your roll with d20 + COU to enter or remain. When an opponent who succeeds on such a roll, it removes 1 from the stake.
- **Benediction** *(Social action, stake 1).* Bless one ally who can hear you. They become Steeled and have advantage on COU checks while the skill is staked. When initiated, one level of Fear is removed from the character.
- **Lictor** _(Utility, stake 1)._ Deputize one ally as your Lictor. While the stake holds:
	- Vestments of Office protects them as it protects you.
	- Any Pronouncements you make are stated by the Lictor in your name applying to anyone you would have influence over, regardless of where the Lictor is.
	- When someone breaks your Pronouncement within their sight, the Lictor make the opposed roll in your place using your WIS.
- **Amendment** _(Free, spend 1, once per round)._ Reword your active Pronouncement without ending it. The stake stays. You may also add Authority to the stake to raise the Sentence mid-scene (e.g. from *Infamia* to *Exilium*), but never lower it. The rewording follows every normal Pronouncement rule, including the Neutrality loss for targeting one group.  
- **Merit or Murder** _(Utility, spend 1)._ When a character is Downed or killed in your presence, or when you examine a corpse no older than a day old you declare if it was done with Merit or Murder. You declare which it was.
    - **Merit:** you determine the killing was justified or otherwise lawful. The killer gains no Scrutiny and cannot become Disgraced for it. Anyone under your influence who seeks revenge for it this scene counts as violating your active Pronouncement. If you do not have an active Pronouncement, they become Disgraced.
    - **Murder:** you determine the death to be murder and the killer immediately suffers your active Pronouncement’s Sentence, with no roll. With no Pronouncement active, they become Disgraced.
    - A ruling that favors your own party costs 2 Neutrality unless the facts plainly support it.

### Tier 4: High Justiciar
- **Codex** _(Passive)._ You may keep two Pronouncements active at once, each staked separately.
- **Ex Post Facto** _(Social action, stake as Pronouncement, lose 5 Neutrality)._ Make a Pronouncement that also applies all actions taken done since the start of your last turn. Anyone under your influence whose action would have violated the Pronouncement makes the roll for violating it. On a failure or critical failure they suffer the Sentence. A success avoids the Sentence and reduces the stake and punishment by one tier. A critical success avoids the Sentence and removes the stake entirely, ending the Pronouncement.
- **Binding Arbitration** *(Utility, spend 2).* When you mediate between two factions in a Scene, a successful Social Front objective you complete advances the Scene Clock one additional step, and neither side may reopen the matter without becoming Disgraced.
- **Absolution** _(Social action, spend 2)._ Remove one effect from a character: a Sentence, Disgraced, every level of Fear, or the Bane of a broken Oath. If the character is an enemy who has surrendered to you, they also become Swayed toward the party for the rest of the scene, and you gain 1 Neutrality.

### Tier 5: Voice of the Compact

- **Canon** _(Utility)._ At the end of a scene, a Pronouncement that is in effect can become Canon. Instead of ending, it becomes standing law in that setting.
	- It keeps its wording and Sentence.
	- It binds the people your Neutrality reached when you made it into Canon, even if your neutrality increases or decreases later.
	- Each standing Canon lowers your Authority cap by 2. You may repeal this at any time which removes the Authority cap.  
 - **Pax Alba** *(Passive).* Any character you have influence over that attack you or your Lictor suffers the active Pronouncement sentence. If there is no Pronouncement active, they suffer *Infamia* and take the Disgraced status. Any character you don't have influence over has disadvantage on their first attack against you in a scene.
 - **Damnatio Memoriae** _(Social action, stake 4)._ Strike a person’s standing from the record. Among everyone under your influence:
	 - their titles, offices and House claims are void
	- their CAS is reduced to as 0
	- they cannot Command, Pull Rank, or call for a duel
	- every Oath sworn to them is released  
	The target may contest it once, with a d20 + CAS against your d20 + WIS. If they succeed, you lose the entire stake and they have advantage on all challenge rolls against you going forward. 

### Playing a Justiciar

Your power comes from your Authority and Neutrality. As you advance, your Authority can give you great power to control the outcome of an engagement, but that power must be wielded carefully. If you favor your own party or try to further your own machinations too obviously, your Neutrality, and therefore your influence, wanes. Justiciars who are without reproach in their unbiased rulings have the ability to reach almost anyone. Ones with clear agendas have almost no power at all.

What Justiciars lack the direct power of pulseFists and swords, they make up for in the indirect weaponization of honor and the use of the full might of the State behind their decrees. An opponent might be bold, but are they bold enough to risk getting hunted down by an Olympic Knight?

Justiciars have several natural synergies that play well within a party. They naturally pair well with Wardogs and Bladesmen who can make the most of the Justiciar's ability to use Verdict to call duels and assign Lictors. They also pair well with classes who might have workarounds to their own Pronouncements. "No blade may be drawn here" isn't going to apply to a Beastmaster with a team of carved beasts after all. 

Outside of combat Justiciars also synergize well with Socially focused allies, with Registrars being the strongest synergy. A Justiciar and Registrar can keep the party out of trouble and, at high tiers, can level a political opponent to irrelevance. A Justiciar's ability to perceive lies makes them fast friends with all interrogation and spy-based classes as well.

On the other hand, Justiciars will have complications with parties including political bulls-in-a-china-shop. Parties that go in hot and loud are going to burn your Neutrality up when you have to bail them out. 

And lastly, a Justiciars biggest weakness is being in a world of lawlessness. Their words carry no weight where the law carries no weight. Missions outside of Society or Republic control, or against factions that clearly have no respect for the rule of law are poor fits for a Justiciar and will leave them as more of a hindrance than a help in many cases.
